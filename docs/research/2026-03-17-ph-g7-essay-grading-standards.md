# Philippine Grade 7 Essay Writing: Grading Standards & Research

> Research compiled: 2026-03-17
> Purpose: Inform recalibration of the ReadTrack grading system for PH G7 context

---

## 1. Curriculum Framework

Grade 7 is under the **MATATAG Curriculum** (phased rollout began SY 2024–2025). This replaces the K-12 CG for affected grade levels.

- [MATATAG English CG Grades 4 & 7 (DepEd PDF)](https://www.deped.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/MATATAG-ENGLISH-CG-G4-and-7.pdf)
- [MATATAG Filipino CG Grades 4 & 7 (DepEd PDF)](https://www.deped.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/MATATAG-FILIPINO-CG-Grades-4-at-7-.pdf)
- [DepEd Order 8, s. 2015 — Classroom Assessment Policy](https://www.deped.gov.ph/2015/04/01/do-8-s-2015-policy-guidelines-on-classroom-assessment-for-the-k-to-12-basic-education-program/)

## 2. Assessment Weight Distribution (DepEd Order 8, s. 2015)

For Language subjects (English and Filipino):

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Written Work | 30% |
| **Performance Tasks** (essays, compositions) | **50%** |
| Quarterly Assessment | 20% |

Essays fall under **Performance Tasks** — the highest-weighted component.

**Minimum passing grade:** Raw 60 = transmuted 75 on the report card.

---

## 3. The DepEd 5-Dimension Rubric

DepEd does not mandate a single national essay rubric. However, the **consensus across DepEd-affiliated resources and academic research** consistently uses these five dimensions:

| # | Dimension | What is assessed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Content** | Relevance, depth of ideas, supporting details, topic development |
| 2 | **Organization** | Clear intro-body-conclusion, logical flow, paragraph structure, transitions |
| 3 | **Language / Vocabulary** | Word choice, register appropriateness, formal language, no Taglish in Filipino essays |
| 4 | **Grammar / Usage** | Verb forms, subject-verb agreement, sentence variety, correct Filipino verb focus (mag-, um-, -in, -an, i-) |
| 5 | **Mechanics** | Capitalization, punctuation, spelling |

**Scoring scales in practice:** 4-point or 5-point analytic scale per dimension.

> **For Filipino-language essays**, criteria 3 and 4 additionally assess:
> - Correct use of Filipino verb focus system (actor/object/locative)
> - Formal Filipino vocabulary (avoiding code-switching)
> - Avoidance of Taglish register

## 4. Grade 7 English Writing: Expected Competencies (MATATAG)

**Performance Standard:** Students evaluate claims in texts and produce culture-based narrative, expository, and persuasive texts appropriate to purpose, context, and audience.

**Text types at G7:**
- Narrative (personal and literary)
- Expository / Informational
- Persuasive / Argumentative
- Précis, summary, paraphrase (from Philippine literature)
- Informative essay (Q4 Module 8)

**Language competencies at G7:**
- Simple, compound, and complex sentences
- Age-appropriate and gender-sensitive language
- Classification of text types (narrative, expository, explanation, recount, persuasive)

---

## 5. Grade 7 Filipino Writing: Expected Competencies (MATATAG)

**Performance Standard:** Learners analyze and produce appropriate oral and written texts using correct grammar, effective organization, and suitable language for varied purposes and audiences, applying critical and creative thinking.

**Writing competencies:**
- Talata (paragraph), sanaysay (essay), maikling kuwento (short story)
- Texts that reflect Philippine culture and identity
- Correct Filipino grammar (verb focus system, verb forms, punctuation)
- Clear intro-body-conclusion structure
- Appropriate vocabulary and register for formal writing

## 6. Research Findings: PH G7 Student Writing Baseline

### 6a. Coherence is the Deepest Problem
**Source:** "Struggling for Coherence" (Ada & Funa, 2025) — 131 G7/G8 students, Eastern Visayas

- Mean coherence score: **M = 2.26 / 5.00 (SD = 0.45)** — Poor to Fair
- Lowest-scoring item: "ideas are very difficult to follow" (M = 1.73)
- Male students significantly underperform vs. female
- Most instruction focuses on surface mechanics; organizational logic is neglected

**Implication:** An essay with coherent flow is performing above average for this population.

### 6b. Mechanics Are the Most Common Error Category
**Source:** "Common Informative Writing Errors" (IJSRA, 2024) — Junior High School PH

Error frequency ranking:
1. Mechanical errors (most common overall)
2. Punctuation (most frequent single error)
3. Word choice / inappropriate register
4. Spelling
5. Preposition misuse
6. Capitalization
7. Verb tense errors
8. Articles (Filipino has no equivalent — major L1 transfer error)
9. Pluralization
10. Pronoun reference
11. Word form errors
12. Subject-verb agreement (least frequent)

**Implication:** Mechanical errors should be the least penalizing dimension for G7, not the most. Grading that heavily weights mechanics will systematically underrate PH G7 students.

### 6c. Formal Filipino Writing Lags Oral Skills
**Source:** "Challenges in Formal Filipino Writing Among Ninth-Grade Students" (JELM, 2024)

- 70% of students have serious problems writing Filipino essays (per teacher reports)
- Students are more orally fluent in Filipino than in written Filipino
- Students write primarily for extrinsic motivation (grades)
- Main problem areas: grammar, coherence, vocabulary, essay structure
- MTB-MLE transition: students only have ~3 years of Filipino-medium instruction before G7

### 6d. Basic Writing Skills Baseline
**Source:** "Improving the Basic Writing Skills of Grade 7 Learners in Filipino" (ERIC EJ1300857, 2021)

Persistent deficiencies at G7 entry level:
- Cursive writing mechanics
- Capitalization and punctuation
- Spelling
- Paragraphing (logical segmentation)

An 80% improvement rate was achieved through targeted drills and parental involvement.

## 7. How Filipino Student Writing Differs from Western Standards

### 7a. Language Transfer Issues
- **No article system in Filipino** → systematic article omission/misuse in English essays
- **Verb-focus morphology** (mag-, um-, -in, -an, i-) has no Western equivalent; mastery is expected at G7
- **Taglish** is the default register in daily communication; formal written Filipino requires explicit register-switching

### 7b. Organization and Coherence
- PH writing education historically prioritizes **surface mechanics over organizational logic**
- Filipino essays tend toward **topic-driven structure** (write everything known about a topic) rather than **claim + evidence** argumentation
- Western rubrics expecting thesis-first, explicit argumentation will systematically underrate Filipino essays

### 7c. Mother Tongue Transition Effects
- MTB-MLE taught K–Grade 3 in regional language (not Filipino or English)
- Students transition to Filipino medium from Grade 4
- G7 students have only ~3 years of Filipino-medium writing instruction
- Proficiency gaps persist into JHS for non-Tagalog region students

### 7d. Cultural Writing Conventions
- Filipino essay tradition values **narrative and anecdote-heavy argumentation**, community experience, and deference to authority as rhetorical support
- MATATAG frames writing tasks around "Philippine culture and identity"
- Personal reflection essays (like the one that prompted this research) are a core and valued genre in PH G7 Filipino

### 7e. DepEd Grading Calibration
- PH teachers calibrate rubric scoring toward PH norms, not Western academic norms
- A communicatively adequate essay with imperfect grammar is typically rated 3/5 on language
- The transmutation system (raw 60 = passing 75) means G7 students can pass with partial competency

## 8. Calibrated G7 Grading Expectations

| Dimension | G7 Passing (3/5) | G7 Excellent (5/5) | Common Deficits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Content** | On-topic with 2–3 supporting details | Deep, culturally situated, well-supported | Thin development, vague ideas |
| **Organization** | Clear 3-part structure | Smooth transitions, strong paragraph unity | Incoherent flow (mean 2.26/5 nationally) |
| **Language** | Formal register, some Taglish avoided | Rich vocabulary, precise register | Code-switching, limited vocabulary |
| **Grammar** | Mostly correct verb forms, minor errors | Consistent accuracy | Tense errors, article errors (in English) |
| **Mechanics** | Mostly correct punctuation/spelling | Near-perfect mechanics | Punctuation most common error type |

## 9. Implications for ReadTrack Grading System

### Current Problems
1. **Grammar over-weighted**: Currently 40% of the score. Should be one of 5 equal dimensions.
2. **No DepEd rubric breakdown**: System outputs a single `natScore`, not the 5-dimension breakdown teachers use.
3. **No language mode**: Filipino essays go through the same grammar checker as English, flagging Filipino structures as errors.
4. **No G7 PH baseline calibration**: The grading thresholds don't reflect that a nationally average PH G7 essay scores ~2.26/5 on coherence.
5. **Feedback is generic**: Currently just `"Rated as {proficiency}. Grammar Accuracy: {x}%."` — not useful for teachers.
6. **Coherence under-measured**: No transition/flow analysis; organization is proxied by structural features only.

### Required Changes
1. Add `DepEdRubricScore` type (5 dimensions, each scored 1–5)
2. Add Gemini-powered rubric evaluation with G7 PH context and language mode
3. New backend endpoint `POST /analyze/rubric` accepting `gradeLevel`, `language`, `text`
4. Update `analyzeStudentWorkAPI` to pass subject `language` and `gradeLevel` to backend
5. Update `EssayViewerModal` analysis tab to display 5-dimension rubric cards
6. Calibrate `svm_models.py` natScore scaling to treat Filipino essays appropriately
